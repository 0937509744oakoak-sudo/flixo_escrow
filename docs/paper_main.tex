\begin{abstract}
Consumer-to-consumer (C2C) escrow platforms fail when buyers and sellers
coordinate payments outside the platform---sharing bank accounts, PromptPay
identifiers, phone numbers, or Line contacts---thereby defeating escrow
protection. Cascaded moderation (cheap local regex, then on-demand LLM) is
attractive under latency and token-cost constraints, but a \emph{cascade gap}
arises when Tier~1 under-scores evasive Thai bait and never escalates.
We present a three-tier hybrid with \emph{soft-trigger} escalation: linguistic
and structural cues (payment-confirmation bait, OCR-distorted PromptPay,
account-holder phrasing, and anonymized-PII stand-ins) force Tier~2 even when
the regex score is below the nominal escalate threshold. We evaluate on a Thai
fraud-chat corpus of $N{=}333$ utterances OCR-extracted from 100 confirmed
fraud screenshots (270~\textsc{allow}, 63~\textsc{block}), labeled by 100\%
human adjudication. In a live 3-way ablation, soft-triggered cascade reaches
93.4\% accuracy with \textbf{recall 0.952} and F1~0.845 (FN$=3$), versus
legacy cascade recall 0.746 (FN$=16$) and regex-only recall 0.714 (FN$=18$),
while still exiting at Tier~1 for 70.6\% of messages (70.6\% API-call saving
versus LLM-on-every-message). Soft trigger rescues 13 false negatives relative
to legacy cascade (7 via live LLM, 6 via Tier~3 soft fail-safe). We do
\emph{not} claim an accuracy win over high-precision regex-only; the
contribution is closing the cascade gap on recall under production cost
constraints.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
C2C escrow, chat moderation, cascaded inference, Thai NLP, fraud detection,
cost-aware AI systems.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

%% =============================================================================
\section{Introduction}
%% =============================================================================

\subsection{Motivation}
Escrow-mediated C2C marketplaces hold buyer funds until delivery conditions are
met. This safety property collapses when counterparties move the conversation
\emph{off-platform}: requesting direct bank transfer, PromptPay top-up, or
Line/phone contact. In Thai peer marketplaces---especially Facebook Marketplace
and Messenger-mediated deals---such circumvention is common, often culminating
in non-delivery after a successful off-platform transfer.

A deployed escrow product therefore needs \emph{in-chat detection} of
off-platform payment and contact bait, with production constraints: low
false-alarm rate (so legitimate bargaining is not blocked), low latency, and
bounded cloud cost.

\subsection{Empirical Insight from Real Fraud Screenshots}
We collected 100 real Thai C2C fraud chat screenshots from public warning
communities and Marketplace-related cases. Optical character recognition (OCR)
yielded $N{=}333$ conversational utterances. Fraud dialogues rarely begin with
slangy ``scam language.'' Instead, approximately 85\% of early turns resemble a
\emph{normal trade flow}---price inquiry, condition questions, shipping
logistics---while risky intent concentrates in later turns that share account
numbers, PromptPay IDs, slips, or Line handles.

This temporal structure motivates cascading: most messages should be decided
locally at near-zero cost; only ambiguous mid-risk text should invoke an LLM.

\subsection{Limitations of Extremes}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Regex-only.} Pattern matching on Thai off-platform keywords,
        phones, and account formats is fast and highly precise when a strong cue
        is present. Soft or fragmented bait---and OCR noise---produce false
        negatives (FN), reducing recall.
  \item \textbf{LLM-always.} Sending every message to a cloud LLM improves
        contextual coverage but incurs high latency and API cost, which is
        wasteful when ${\sim}$80\% of turns are routine inquiry.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
This paper makes three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Cascade-gap diagnosis.} We show that a score-gated cascade
        under-escalates OCR fraud bait (payment-confirm phrases and redacted
        contact/account tokens), so legacy hybrid barely improves recall over
        regex-only on this corpus.
  \item \textbf{Soft-triggered architecture.} A production 3-tier moderator
        (regex~$\rightarrow$~multi-provider LLM~$\rightarrow$~graceful fallback)
        with soft escalation cues that do \emph{not} hard-block by themselves,
        but force Tier~2 (and inform Tier~3 fail-safe).
  \item \textbf{Live 3-way ablation.} Regex-only vs.\ cascade (soft~OFF) vs.\
        cascade+soft (proposed) on $N{=}333$ human-labeled utterances, reporting
        detection metrics, McNemar tests, FN rescue counts, latency, and
        projected token cost---without fabricated statistics.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Proposed Cascaded Architecture}
%% =============================================================================

We denote a message $x$ and a binary risk decision
$y\in\{\mathrm{ALLOW},\mathrm{BLOCK}\}$, where production verdicts
\texttt{flag}/\texttt{reject} map to \textsc{block} and \texttt{pass} maps to
\textsc{allow}.

\subsection{Tier 1 --- Local Regex and Heuristics}
Tier~1 normalizes Thai/Latin text and scores cues including: Thai mobile and
international phone patterns; bank-account digit formats and bank-name keywords;
PromptPay and off-platform transfer phrases; Line ID / social contact patterns;
and discount$+$bypass bait conjunctions.

A risk score $s_{\mathrm{regex}}\in[0,100]$ is mapped to verdicts with production
thresholds (flag~$\geq 45$, block/reject~$\geq 70$). These cutoffs, together with
the escalation bounds below, are \emph{empirical prototype defaults} chosen by
engineering intuition during development; they were \emph{not} tuned on a held-out
split of this corpus (Section~\ref{sec:eval-protocol}).

\subsection{Tier 2 --- Cloud LLM Multi-Provider Escalation}
Escalation triggers when AI moderation is enabled and any of the following hold,
with defaults $s_{\min}{=}25$ and $s_{\mathrm{skip}}{=}80$:
\begin{equation}
s_{\mathrm{regex}}\in[s_{\min}, s_{\mathrm{skip}})
\quad\text{or}\quad
\text{suspicious digit run}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{soft}}(x)\neq\emptyset,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{soft}}$ is the soft-cue set defined below. Scores
$s_{\mathrm{regex}}\geq s_{\mathrm{skip}}$ skip the LLM (Tier~1 already
decisive).

Providers are tried in order: \textbf{Groq~$\rightarrow$~OpenRouter~$\rightarrow$~Gemini}.
The live ablation used free-tier endpoints with production default model IDs:
\texttt{llama-3.3-70b-versatile} (Groq), \texttt{google/gemini-2.5-flash}
(OpenRouter), and \texttt{gemini-2.0-flash} (Gemini). Decoding uses
temperature~$0.1$ and JSON-object response format (\texttt{max\_tokens}$=2048$).
The prompt asks the model to score only off-platform circumvention cues (bank /
PromptPay identifiers, phone or Line contacts, external links, and solicitations
to pay outside escrow) and to return JSON with
\texttt{risk\_score}$\in[0,100]$,
\texttt{verdict}$\in\{\texttt{pass},\texttt{flag},\texttt{reject}\}$,
\texttt{reasons} (short Thai strings), and
\texttt{categories}$\in\{\texttt{bank\_account},\texttt{phone\_contact},
\texttt{off\_platform\_transfer},\texttt{external\_link},\texttt{none}\}$.
Invalid JSON, empty responses, authentication errors, and timeouts are treated as
provider failures and fall through the chain; if all providers fail, Tier~3
returns the Tier~1 decision unless soft cues or digit-run heuristics raise
\texttt{pass}$\rightarrow$\texttt{flag} (fail-safe).

Results are merged conservatively with Tier~1 via
$\max(s_{\mathrm{regex}}, s_{\mathrm{llm}})$ and a worse-verdict operator
(\texttt{reject}$\succ$\texttt{flag}$\succ$\texttt{pass}): the LLM cannot
\emph{downgrade} a high regex score. This policy is intentionally
false-negative-averse (prefer over-flagging to missing a bypass), at the cost of
additional false positives.

\subsection{Soft-Trigger Escalation (Proposed Gate)}
\label{sec:soft-trigger}
Legacy score-gating only escalates when $s_{\mathrm{regex}}\geq s_{\min}$ (or a
long digit run). On this OCR corpus, many \textsc{block} utterances score
$0$---e.g., ``โอนแล้วแจ้งสลิป'' (transfer-done / send-slip bait) and messages
whose strong identifiers were replaced by
\texttt{[PHONE\_NUM]}/\texttt{[BANK\_ACCOUNT]}/\texttt{[LINE\_ID]}---so the LLM
never runs. Soft triggers close this gap \emph{without} treating the cues as
hard regex blocks (which would inflate FP on benign slip talk). Cue families:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Payment-confirm bait:} Thai phrases such as transfer-done /
        send-slip / confirm-slip patterns.
  \item \textbf{Anonymized PII stand-ins:} evaluation placeholders that mirror
        digit/contact patterns removed by redaction.
  \item \textbf{Account-holder phrasing} and OCR-distorted PromptPay spellings.
  \item \textbf{Soft phone contact:} ``เบอร์''/``โทร'' near digits or phone
        placeholders; spaced bank labels (e.g., ``K Bank'').
\end{itemize}
Soft cues force Tier~2; if the provider chain fails, Tier~3 may raise to
\texttt{flag} when cues are present (availability-preserving fail-safe).

\subsection{Tier 3 --- Graceful Degradation}
If all providers fail, the system returns the Tier~1 decision and optionally
raises \texttt{pass}$\rightarrow$\texttt{flag} on digit-run or soft-cue
heuristics, preserving chat availability under cloud outage.

\subsection{Gold Labels}
Screenshots were collected from public Thai anti-scam community pages that
document confirmed fraud cases (conversation-level fraud is therefore 100\%).
Utterance-level gold labels were assigned by a \emph{single human annotator}
(the author) who read every OCR utterance in screenshot context. Coverage is
100\% of the $N{=}333$ retained utterances. The rubric is independent of the
production Tier~1 regex/heuristic detector:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textsc{allow}: ordinary marketplace inquiry, bargaining, or logistics.
  \item \textsc{block}: sharing a bank account, PromptPay ID, phone number, or
        Line contact, or soliciting a transfer outside escrow.
\end{itemize}
Because labeling was performed by one annotator, inter-annotator agreement (IAA)
is not reported; this remains a limitation. Residual OCR/UI noise can still
distort the \emph{input text} even when the intended label is correct.

\subsection{Systems Compared}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Regex-only:} Tier~1 only
        (\texttt{scanTextRegexOnly}).
  \item \textbf{Cascade (no soft):} full cascade with soft trigger disabled
        (legacy score/digit gate only).
  \item \textbf{Cascade+Soft (proposed):} full cascade with soft-trigger
        escalation enabled (\texttt{scanText} with
        \texttt{soft\_trigger=true}).
\end{itemize}
Binary mapping: $\{\texttt{flag},\texttt{reject}\}\!\rightarrow\!\textsc{block}$,
$\{\texttt{pass}\}\!\rightarrow\!\textsc{allow}$. Gold labels were \emph{not}
derived from this detector, so regex-only is not scored against its own rules.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:eval-protocol}
This study is a production-path evaluation, not a trained classifier: there is
no parameter learning. Thresholds
($s_{\min}{=}25$, $s_{\mathrm{skip}}{=}80$, flag~$\geq 45$, reject~$\geq 70$)
are empirical defaults from prototype development and were \emph{not} selected
on a development split of this corpus. Soft-cue patterns were designed from
cascade-gap failure analysis on the prior live run (FN with
$s_{\mathrm{regex}}{=}0$), then re-evaluated in a fresh 3-way ablation on the
same $N{=}333$ set; we treat held-out cue/threshold calibration as future work.
Runner: \texttt{scripts/run\_chat\_benchmark\_ablation.php -{}-mode=live}.

\subsection{Metrics}
Detection: accuracy, precision, recall, F1, Cohen's $\kappa$. Significance:
McNemar tests on paired per-utterance correctness (regex vs.\ soft; cascade vs.\
soft). Effect-size narrative: FN rescued and FP introduced between systems.
Efficiency: Tier~1 exit rate, mean/P95 latency, and \emph{projected} LLM-token
cost versus an LLM-every-message counterfactual. Google Cloud Vision OCR is a
fixed corpus-construction cost, not a cascade operating cost. USD figures use
blended \$/1M rates and a character-based token proxy (research estimate, not
invoices).

\paragraph{Cascade gap without soft triggers.}
Regex-only is highly precise (FP$=1$, Precision$=0.978$) but misses many
\textsc{block} cases (FN$=18$, Recall$=0.714$). Legacy cascade barely moves
recall ($0.714\!\rightarrow\!0.746$) while adding FP ($1\!\rightarrow\!15$) and
\emph{hurting} accuracy ($94.3\%\!\rightarrow\!90.7\%$; McNemar $p{=}0.006$
\emph{against} cascade). Soft triggers are therefore not a cosmetic add-on:
without them the cascade is a poor accuracy trade on this corpus.

\paragraph{Soft trigger closes the gap.}
Cascade+Soft lifts recall to $0.952$ (FN $16\!\rightarrow\!3$) and F1 to
$0.845$, recovering \textbf{13} false negatives relative to legacy cascade
(7 decided by live Tier~2 LLM; 6 by Tier~3 soft fail-safe when providers
failed). Residual FN$=3$ are soft payment-confirm phrases where the LLM (or
merge) still returned \textsc{allow}. McNemar cascade$\rightarrow$soft is
borderline ($p{=}0.081$); we emphasize the effect size (FN $-81\%$,
recall $+20.6$\,pp) rather than a significance claim at $\alpha{=}0.05$.

\paragraph{Accuracy versus operational risk.}
Cascade+Soft does \emph{not} beat regex-only on accuracy ($93.4\%$ vs.\
$94.3\%$; McNemar $p{=}0.728$). The trade is intentional for escrow safety:
missing a bypass (FN) is costlier than a false alarm (FP). Soft trigger buys
near-complete \textsc{block} coverage at the cost of FP $1\!\rightarrow\!19$.

Soft trigger escalates more often (Tier1 $77.5\%\!\rightarrow\!70.6\%$) but still
saves 70.6\% of API calls versus LLM-every, with projected token cost
\$0.0083 versus \$0.0474 (${\approx}82\%$ saving under the proxy rates). Mean
latency rises modestly (492\,ms$\rightarrow$549\,ms). Scaled to 1M messages,
the soft cascade remains ${\sim}$5$\times$ cheaper than always-on LLM under the
same proxy---the systems motivation for cascading.

\subsection{Failure and Trade-off Analysis}
\paragraph{False positives (Soft FP$=19$).}
LLM over-flags ambiguous logistics / OCR chrome; soft payment-confirm cues also
escalate benign ``send slip'' talk that is legitimate \emph{inside} escrow.
Tier~3 soft fail-safe can contribute FP when providers fail.

\paragraph{False negatives (Soft FN$=3$).}
All remaining misses are payment-confirm bait that soft-escalated but were not
blocked after LLM/merge---prompt and merge calibration targets for future work.

\paragraph{Design takeaway.}
The research question is not ``does an LLM beat regex on accuracy?'' but
\emph{when} should a cascade call the LLM. Soft triggers answer that for Thai
OCR fraud chat: escalate low-score bait that regex under-scores, accept more FP
to slash FN, keep ${\sim}$70\% of traffic on Tier~1.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Single annotator.} Gold is 100\% human-labeled by one author;
        IAA is unavailable.
  \item \textbf{Cue design on observed gaps.} Soft patterns were motivated by
        prior FN analysis on this corpus; held-out cue discovery is future work.
  \item \textbf{Provider instability.} This live run had non-zero Tier~3
        fallbacks; 6/13 FN rescues used soft fail-safe rather than a successful
        LLM JSON verdict---we report both sources explicitly.
  \item \textbf{Anonymization stand-ins.} \texttt{[PHONE\_NUM]} etc.\ are
        evaluation proxies for redacted identifiers; production digit/contact
        regex would often fire on the unreacted text.
  \item \textbf{No held-out threshold tuning;} utterance-level only;
        LLM variability not repeated; domain limited to Thai anti-scam
        Facebook/Messenger screenshots; cost figures are projected proxies.
\end{enumerate}

%% =============================================================================
\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
%% =============================================================================

We identified a cascade gap in score-gated Thai escrow chat moderation and
closed it with soft-trigger escalation. On $N{=}333$ live-evaluated OCR
utterances, Cascade+Soft reaches recall $0.952$ and F1 $0.845$ (FN$=3$),
rescuing 13 misses versus legacy cascade while retaining a 70.6\% Tier~1 exit
rate. We do not claim an accuracy win over high-precision regex-only; we claim
a production-relevant recall recovery under bounded cloud cost.

Future work: held-out soft-cue/threshold calibration; merge/prompt ablations to
cut FP; second-annotator IAA; conversation-level early-warning metrics; and
repeated LLM trials under stable paid quotas.
